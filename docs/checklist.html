Learner-First UX/LX Checklist

Purpose · Severity levels · Part 1: User Experience (UX) · Part 2: Learner Experience (LX) · References

Created by Chelsea Taylor · Licensed CC BY 4.0

Purpose

The purpose of this checklist is to measure the user and learner experience across digital learning content, such as eLearning course exports and slide decks.

  • User Experience is the extent to which a user, regardless of impairment, can understand and interact with the content.
  • Learner Experience is the extent to which a learner, regardless of impairment, can achieve learning outcomes.

Severity levels

The test for every level: how does this impact the learner? It keeps the focus on the learner and their experience, not on design or speed.

#LevelDefinitionExampleLaunch
1BlockedThe learner can't access the content.Video with no captions and no transcript; a lesson or page with no contentMust be fixed before launch
2InaccurateThe content is wrong, and a learner who follows it could make a mistake on the job. Wrong high-stakes content is always Inaccurate.A wrong fee amountMust be fixed before launch
3DetractsThe learner gets less: less of the content, or a worse version of it than other learners get.Transcript but no captionsFix before launch, or launch with a named owner and a fix date
4Adds effortThe learner gets the same content but has to work harder for it, which can hurt retention depending on subject, familiarity, distraction and pressure.A misspelling that makes them re-readCan launch; fix in the next revision
5Minor errorIncorrect, but doesn't block understanding. Each one wears down the learner's trust in the content, and in the designer.A missing comma; a wrong founding yearCan launch; fix in the next revision

High-stakes content (wrong content here is always Inaccurate, in any module):

  • Money: prices, fees, rates, amounts, billing steps
  • Legal & compliance: regulations, policy, contract terms, how personal data is handled
  • Safety & security: physical safety, health, passwords, access, identity checks and fraud
  • Client-facing: anything the learner says, sends or shows to a customer
  • Can't be undone: steps like approve, submit, delete, send

Rules:

  • Each finding gets ONE level, the most severe that applies. Other levels it hits go in an Also column.
  • Severity sets the suggested fix order. Within a level, findings with more Also entries sort first.
  • The tool suggests the fix order; the reviewer and the designer can override it.
  • Blocked and Detracts never move because content is high-stakes.
  • Text that fails contrast (item 1) is Detracts: low-vision learners get a worse version of the same content. Text under 2:1 is Blocked: it is too faint to read at all.
  • Missing required information (an amount, a step, a setting) is Inaccurate when following the course without it could lead to a mistake on the job. When the content is only less complete, it is Detracts.
  • A large pile of Minor error findings can raise severity, but only rarely. No threshold set.

Who checks:

  • File: the tool answers it from the course files.
  • Draft: the tool drafts an answer, marked unconfirmed; a human confirms.
  • Human: left open for a person.
  • If the tool ever grades long-form answers, a person confirms the grade.

Practice and feedback

#Short nameCriterionWho checksReference
